Grammatik — Präsens & Verbklammer

Regel 1 — Verb an Position 2

The conjugated verb is the second element, always. Not the second word — the second element. Anything can occupy position 1, and the subject then slides behind the verb.

Position 1VerbRest
Ichfahremorgen nach Wien.
Morgenfahreich nach Wien.
Nach Wienfahreich morgen.
Mit meiner Fraufahreich morgen nach Wien.

Common Anglophone error: Morgen ich fahre… — English lets you front an adverb without inverting. German doesn't.

Regel 2 — Die Satzklammer

The second verb part goes to the very end, bracketing the sentence. Everything else piles up inside the bracket.

Ichrufedich morgenan
Trennbares Verb — anrufen
Ichmussmorgen frühaufstehen
Modalverb — Infinitiv am Ende
Ichhabedich gesternangerufen
Perfekt — Partizip am Ende

Selbstcheck

Answer without looking back, then reveal.

Why is Gestern ich habe gearbeitet wrong?

Position 1 is filled by Gestern, so the verb must sit in position 2 and the subject moves behind it: Gestern habe ich gearbeitet. Fronting an adverb without inverting is the classic English-speaker's V2 error.

Where does the prefix of a separable verb go in a main clause? In a question?

To the very end of the clause, closing the Satzklammer — in a statement (Ich rufe dich morgen an.), in a Ja/Nein-question (Kannst du mir helfen? — well, that one's modal+infinitive, but the shape holds), and in a W-question (Wann fängt der Kurs an?). Only the finite verb moves to position 1/2; the prefix always stays last.

Which three verbs must you know perfectly before anything else works?

sein, haben, werden — they carry the Perfekt, the passive, the future, and half the idioms in the language. Automatic recall, no hesitation.
